# The Salvage Kings Loot Manifesto

*Salvage Kings is derived from the classic Suicide Kings strategy and named for the bottom-of-the-list "salvager" role. "Suicide" below still means dropping to the bottom after a win.*

*We are uncovering better ways of sharing raid loot by doing it together.*
Through this work we have come to value:

### In‑the‑moment decisions — over — plans made before the raid
For many of us, the live call when something drops is a big part of the fun — so we skip wishlists,
drop‑table homework, and decisions locked in beforehand.

### Individual agency — over — central authority
We'd rather each player judge what a drop is worth to them and make their own call — not a loot
council, officers, or an algorithm deciding for them.

### Earned turns — over — luck of the roll
We'd rather loot distribute evenly across everyone who shows up than leave it to pure chance — which
often produces hot and cold rollers, and that just feels bad.

### Transparency and social trust — over — rules enforced by penalty
We lean toward making behavior visible and letting the guild hold itself accountable, rather than
policing it with automatic penalties.

### Effortless fairness — over — high‑maintenance optimization
We aim for it to be **hard to be unfair and easy to be fair** — the rules carry the weight, so loot
runs with less debate and drama.

> That is: while there is real value in the items on the right, **we value the items on the left more.**

---

## The principles behind it

1. **A loot system's first job is to be trusted.** Every decision is visible and verifiable, and the record is append‑only and tamper‑evident — so agreement is a fact, not a hope.
2. **Decisions are made live, after the drop** — not pre‑committed on a list.
3. **Your position is your only currency.** You spend it only when you win; passing is always free.
4. **The highest player who wants an item wins it, and pays by returning to the bottom.** One rule, for everyone, every time — easy to follow, hard to game.
5. **An item is worth whatever someone will spend on it** — the standings reveal its value; we don't score or rank it.
6. **Usable loot finds a home, not a shard.** If anyone will take an item, it reaches them — through the draft, the cascade, or the backstop. It's disenchanted only when truly no one wants it.
7. **We surface hoarding rather than punish it.** Your standing changes only when you win — never by decay, absence, or judgment — and the record makes the patterns visible.
8. **Generosity is enabled and seen, never required.** You can give an item to anyone, in any direction; the cost is yours.
9. **Mistakes are fixed in the open.** Corrections are recorded, never hidden; history is never rewritten.
10. **Officers run the process; they don't decide who deserves loot.** The rules do — and the record outlasts memory.
11. **We judge success by fairness and trust, not by how optimally gear was handed out** — a trade we make on purpose.

---

## What this is *not* (honest limitations)

- **Not an optimizer.** It will not guarantee the "best" item reaches the "best" use. It guarantees a fair, transparent process — the outcomes are whatever the guild's live choices produce.
- **Not a replacement for culture.** It makes behavior visible; people still choose how to behave. The tool surfaces hoarding and generosity — it doesn't manufacture either.
- **Not trustless.** It assumes officers act in good faith. The hash‑chained ledger reliably catches *mistakes, dropped messages, and desync* — not a determined bad‑faith officer. We chose a guild's trust model, not a bank's.
